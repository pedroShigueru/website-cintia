Whitening products are available in pharmacies, on marketplaces and in beauty salons. The question is not whether they whiten, but what happens when they are used without anyone having looked inside the mouth first.

What the whitening agent does

Hydrogen peroxide, or the carbamide peroxide that converts into it, passes through the enamel and dentine and breaks down the pigment molecules lodged in the structure of the tooth. The effect is chemical and happens inside the tooth, not on its surface.

That is why whitening removes neither tartar nor surface plaque staining — that is the job of a professional clean, and it is common for part of the dissatisfaction with colour to resolve before any whitening.

What the prior assessment picks up

Three situations change the approach completely, and only show up on clinical examination.

Decay or a leaking restoration. The whitening agent reaches exposed dentine and the pulp by a route that should not exist, which causes intense pain and can damage the pulp.

Gum recession with an exposed root. The root has no enamel. Direct contact between the agent and root dentine greatly increases sensitivity.

Intrinsic causes of discolouration. Teeth darkened by trauma, old root canal treatment or tetracycline respond differently, and sometimes call for another approach, such as internal whitening of one specific tooth.

Concentration and timing are not details

The result depends on the combination of agent concentration and contact time. Over-the-counter products work at low concentrations so they are safe without supervision, which usually means a limited effect.

The opposite path — raising the concentration or the time on your own — is what produces cases of severe sensitivity and gum irritation. Supervision exists to calibrate those two factors against the response observed.

About sensitivity

Some sensitivity during treatment is common and transient. It usually disappears a few days after finishing.

What supervision allows is managing it: applying a desensitiser before and during, spacing the sessions out, reducing the concentration. Without follow-up, the alternative is usually to abandon the treatment halfway.

Restorations do not whiten

Composites, crowns and veneers keep their original colour. If there is a visible restoration on the front teeth, it will probably need replacing after whitening to match the shade.
