When rehabilitation is indicated

Oral rehabilitation treats cases where loss of tooth structure compromises chewing, speech or appearance across the whole mouth. That includes multiple missing teeth, severe wear from bruxism, extensive fractures, and situations where earlier restorations no longer do their job.

Unlike an isolated procedure, rehabilitation starts from a view of the whole: how the teeth relate to one another, how chewing forces are distributed, and how the system will behave after treatment.

Integrated planning

Diagnosis brings together clinical examination, photographs, digital models, imaging where indicated, and a record of the relationship between the arches. From that data the outcome can be designed before any intervention, and the sequence between the specialities involved — periodontics, endodontics, implantology and prosthodontics — can be defined.

This stage also establishes what has to be treated first: active periodontal disease, sources of infection and decay are resolved before the rehabilitative phase.

Stages

  1. 01

    Diagnosis and design

    A full workup, digital planning and a presentation of the treatment options with their stages and timescales.

  2. 02

    Preparatory phase

    Treatment of active conditions and, where needed, grafting, extractions and placement of implants.

  3. 03

    Rehabilitative phase

    Temporary work to validate function and appearance, followed by the definitive prostheses and a maintenance programme.

Temporary work as a validation stage

In extensive rehabilitations, temporary prostheses are not there merely to fill the gap. They allow height, shape and function to be tested for a period before the definitive work is made, and anything that needs changing can be adjusted while correction is still simple.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take?

It varies with the extent of the case and the need for preliminary procedures such as grafting or periodontal treatment. Broad cases usually run over several months, with stages defined from the outset.

Will I be left without teeth at some point?

No. The plan includes temporary prostheses that maintain function and appearance throughout every phase.

Fixed or removable prosthesis on implants?

The choice depends on bone condition, the number of viable implants, functional expectations and the hygiene routine. Both options are presented with their implications before the decision is made.
