What aligner treatment is

Invisalign is a system of clear, removable orthodontic aligners. Instead of fixed brackets, tooth movement is driven by a sequence of custom-made trays, each responsible for one small step of the planned displacement.

Planning starts from an intraoral digital scan, which replaces conventional impressions. From that scan it is possible to view a simulation of the predicted outcome before treatment begins, and to discuss the objectives clearly.

Indications

The system is suitable for a considerable share of cases of crowding, spacing, deep bite, crossbite and relapse after previous orthodontic treatment. Indication depends on clinical examination and imaging: not every case suits aligners, and cases requiring complex movements may respond more predictably to fixed appliances.

How follow-up works

  1. 01

    Assessment and scanning

    Clinical examination, radiographs and intraoral scanning. This is the stage that determines whether the case is suitable for aligners.

  2. 02

    Digital planning

    Step-by-step simulation of tooth movement, with the predicted outcome and the estimated duration presented to you.

  3. 03

    Wear and reviews

    Aligners are changed on schedule, with periodic follow-up appointments and adjustments whenever they are needed.

Looking after the aligners

Aligners must be removed to eat and to brush, and put straight back in afterwards. Hot drinks with the aligner in place should be avoided, as they can deform the material. Cleaning the trays is part of the daily routine and is explained at the appointment.

Once the active phase ends, a retention phase follows to preserve the position achieved. Retention is part of the treatment, not an optional extra.

Frequently asked questions

How long does the treatment take?

It depends on the complexity of the case. Simple alignment usually takes 6 to 9 months; broader cases can run beyond 12 months. The estimate is presented at the assessment, based on the digital plan.

Are the aligners visible when I speak?

They are made of transparent material and are discreet in most social situations, although they can be noticed close up.

Do I have to wear them all day?

The recommended wear is 20 to 22 hours a day, removing the aligners to eat and to brush. Sticking to the wear time has a direct effect on how the treatment progresses.

Does it hurt?

Pressure and sensitivity in the first days of each new aligner are common. The sensation usually eases over the course of the week.
