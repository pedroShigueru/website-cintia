Dental implants on Avenida Paulista

Replacing missing teeth with CBCT planning and guided surgery, for a more predictable and less invasive procedure.

Book an assessment

What a dental implant is

An implant is a titanium post placed in the upper or lower jawbone to replace the root of a lost tooth. A prosthesis is fitted onto it, restoring the shape and function of the missing tooth.

Losing a tooth affects more than appearance: it changes how chewing forces are distributed, can shift neighbouring teeth and leads to progressive resorption of the bone in the region. The implant also works to preserve that structure.

Planning and guided surgery

Before surgery, a cone beam CT scan is taken to assess bone volume and density and the position of anatomical structures such as the maxillary sinuses and nerves. With that data, the position of each implant is defined in software before any intervention.

Where the case allows, surgery is carried out with a surgical guide printed from that plan. This tends to shorten the procedure and reduce the extent of the opening required.

Stages of treatment

  1. 01

    Assessment and CT scan

    Clinical examination, review of general health and a CT scan to confirm that the implant is viable.

  2. 02

    Placement

    Surgery under local anaesthetic, guided by the digital plan. In selected cases, with an immediate temporary prosthesis.

  3. 03

    Osseointegration and prosthesis

    A period of integration between implant and bone, followed by the making and fitting of the definitive prosthesis.

From a single tooth to full rehabilitation

The same principle applies at different scales: a single tooth, a segment with several missing teeth, or a full arch on implants. The choice between a fixed prosthesis and a removable one on implants depends on bone condition, functional expectations and the patient's hygiene routine. Extensive cases are covered in detail on the oral rehabilitation page.

Frequently asked questions

Does the surgery hurt?

The procedure is carried out under local anaesthetic and does not cause pain during surgery. Recovery usually involves discomfort and swelling for a few days, controlled with the prescribed medication.

How long until the definitive prosthesis?

Osseointegration usually takes three to six months, varying with the region and bone quality. In selected cases a temporary prosthesis can be fitted on the same day.

Is there an upper age limit?

There is no upper age limit. What determines the indication is general and oral health, assessed at the initial examination and on imaging.

What if there is not enough bone?

Bone grafting and sinus lift procedures can create the conditions for an implant. The need for them is identified on the CT scan.
