Teeth whitening

Supervised protocols, preceded by a clinical assessment and carried out with sensitivity kept under control.

Book an assessment

Assessment before whitening

Whitening begins with a clinical examination. Untreated decay, leaking restorations, gum recession and pre-existing sensitivity all need to be identified before any application, because they affect both comfort and outcome.

This is also the stage that establishes where the discolouration comes from. Extrinsic stains linked to coffee, tea, wine or tobacco respond differently from intrinsic changes associated with dental ageing, trauma or medication.

Available protocols

In-office whitening uses a higher-concentration agent applied by the clinician in controlled sessions. Supervised take-home whitening uses custom trays and a lower concentration, worn daily over a set period. The two protocols can be combined, depending on the case.

The choice takes into account the degree of discolouration, pre-existing sensitivity and the patient's routine. In both cases, professional supervision is what allows concentration and timing to be adjusted in response to what is observed.

Sensitivity

Transient sensitivity is the most common side effect and usually resolves within a few days of finishing. Preventive measures — desensitising agents applied before and during treatment, and adjusting the interval between sessions — help keep discomfort under control.

Maintaining the result

After whitening, extra care with staining food and drink is advisable in the first few weeks. In the longer term, good hygiene and follow-up appointments determine how long the result holds. Periodic maintenance sessions are an expected part of the process.

Frequently asked questions

Does whitening damage the enamel?

Carried out under professional supervision, with an appropriate concentration and application time, whitening does not cause structural damage to the enamel. The risk is associated with indiscriminate use of products without a prior assessment.

How long does the result last?

It varies with diet, smoking and oral hygiene. Periodic maintenance sessions are commonly needed.

Do fillings and crowns whiten too?

No. Restorative materials do not respond to the whitening agent. Where there are visible restorations, they may need replacing after whitening to match the shade.

Can I whiten during orthodontic treatment?

With removable aligners it is possible in many cases, subject to assessment. With fixed appliances, whitening is usually postponed until after removal, to avoid a colour difference in the covered areas.
