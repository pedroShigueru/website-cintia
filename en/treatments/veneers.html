Ceramic laminates designed from a digital preview, removing as little natural tooth structure as the case allows.

What ceramic laminates are

Ceramic laminates are thin porcelain shells bonded to the visible surface of the tooth. They correct shape, colour, minor misalignment and wear, without changing the position of the teeth in the arch.

The terms "ultra-thin laminate" and "veneer" describe variations of the same treatment, distinguished by the thickness of the material and the amount of preparation required.

Preserving natural structure

The principle guiding this treatment at the clinic is minimal preparation. Whenever the case allows, reduction is limited or unnecessary. Where some preparation is needed so the material does not leave the tooth looking bulky, the extent is defined during planning and presented before treatment starts.

This matters because enamel that is removed does not grow back. A case that needs little preparation today keeps other approaches open for the future.

Digital preview and trial

Before the definitive work is made, the result is designed digitally from photographs and the intraoral scan. In many cases a trial can be carried out directly in the mouth with temporary material, so the proposal can be seen and adjustments to shape and proportion discussed before anything irreversible is done.

When another approach fits better

Laminates do not correct tooth position. Where there is crowding or significant misalignment, treatment with aligners before the laminates usually allows a result with less preparation. Equally, when the concern is colour alone, whitening may resolve it without intervening in the structure of the tooth.

Presenting those alternatives is part of the assessment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an ultra-thin laminate and a veneer?

They are variations of the same treatment. The ultra-thin laminate is a thinner shell, indicated when little or no preparation is needed. A veneer is thicker and is indicated when the case calls for a greater change of shape or colour.

Does the tooth have to be prepared?

It depends on the case. In some situations preparation is minimal or unnecessary, in others some is needed. The extent is defined during planning and presented before treatment starts.

How long do they last?

Longevity depends on the quality of the bonding, the bite and the patient's habits. Untreated bruxism and using the teeth to open packaging are among the main causes of fracture.

Do they stain over time?

Porcelain itself does not absorb pigment. Colour changes noticed over time usually come from the bonding line or from the adjacent natural tooth.
